# Everyday learning, relationship memory and practice outcomes

26 September 2026 · installed local development alpha · bounded verification complete

This increment connects three existing ARCHi surfaces to the evidence they can actually use: exact method-version outcomes in Work together, user-reported relationship records in Memories, and observed solo-action results in Arena. They retain different meanings and authority. Opening a history, reviewing a note or winning practice does not automatically award learning or saved growth.

## Review a method before reusing it

1. In **Work together**, open **Saved procedures** and inspect the method's instruction, version and requirements.
2. Expand **Review uses of v…**. Verified applied records awaiting review appear first. Each entry identifies its assistant, time and request; expand it to see the existing feedback controls and **Usage for this attempt**.
3. Choose **Helpful** or **Needs correction** only when you can verify that outcome from your work. The history retains checks and references, not the passage or reply. Learning review and saved evolution remain separate explicit actions.
4. Follow the preparation hint: choose **Revise**, select a current passage and match the method's requirements. Use **Find a saved method** to search by task, or browse Saved procedures. **Use for this passage** opens a preview; inspect its instruction and the draft it would replace, confirm, then **Send**. A returned proposal still needs its checks, your review and explicit **Apply**. [Task-aware finder](native-method-finder.md).

Counts match the full method ID, revision and digest. A supporting edit does not count as a use of a newly authored method. Proposals, failed requests and incomplete application evidence cannot become Helpful outcomes. Earlier versions keep their own history. A retained counterexample continues to block that version even if a later review changes; a corrective result can support a new version through **Edit method**.

The new view explains the existing reuse rules; it does not loosen them or select a method automatically. ## Keep relationship context and prepare locally

1. Open **Memories → People & follow-through → Keep a note**. Enter a title and your chosen note text, then **Keep note on this Mac**. This retains a source; it does not infer facts about anyone.
2. Choose **Add person…**, author the record and link an exact supporting passage from a kept source. **Save draft**, review its wording and evidence, then **Mark reviewed**.
3. Select that person and choose **Add encounter…** or **Add commitment…**. Link supporting passages and retain the exact reviewed person version. Dates may remain unknown. Save and review each record separately.
4. Select up to three reviewed related records and choose **Prepare locally**. This replaces the previous page selection with that person and those records, prepares local context and opens Chat. Write your preparation question and explicitly send it when ready; preparation alone sends nothing.
5. Use **Revise…**, **Withdraw** or the commitment status controls when something changes. Marking a commitment completed or cancelled creates a new draft that needs review before preparation can use it.

**Show history** opens that record's saved versions directly beneath its controls. **Hide history** closes the view; neither changes the record. Save and preparation feedback also appears in the People card, so a refused action has a visible explanation there.

If **Add person…** is disabled in an empty library, open **Keep a note** and retain your chosen source first. A valid source needs a title and nonempty text. If **Prepare locally** is unavailable, review the current person and selected records and check their source passages; changed dependencies must be explicitly revised and reviewed.

Person, Encounter and Commitment are typed metadata in the existing source/knowledge-page library, with user-reported attribution. Reviewed means eligible for the chosen local use, not independently verified truth. Completed means the user's report, not proof that a message was sent or an external action occurred. This flow does not scan contacts, contact people, schedule reminders or create a separate person database.

Source replacement or forgetting makes dependent anchors unavailable. Changing or withdrawing a person invalidates related records bound to that earlier version. Revise those records with current evidence and explicitly choose the current person before reviewing them again. Prior versions remain history; neither a new name nor newer source text is silently substituted. ## Observe resolved solo actions in Arena

Open **Arena → Play Arena** and use solo practice. While the native session is connected, **Practice outcomes → Recent actions** shows accepted observations: round, chosen and rival actions, damage, Integrity/Spark changes, and any practice result. The compatible Unity player must advertise action-outcome reporting; an older player can acknowledge rendering without providing these records.

The action report is a separate bounded stream from the rendering acknowledgment. Native checks bind its fresh header to the presentation session and version, then check event structure, sequence and numerical consistency. The in-memory consumer rejects rewrites of retained overlapping events, reused action identities, backward cursors and session changes without reset. It retains at most 32 recent observed actions and reports missing sequences. Repeated polls do not add the same event again. Ending the session clears these observations; this is not a persistent or complete play log.

Paired play emits no action receipts in this version. Earlier solo observations may remain visible after switching modes, with an explicit status message; they cannot certify paired actions or results. These are Unity rule outcomes from explicit solo inputs, not physics-contact evidence, autonomous commands, real-world execution, certified skills or canonical companion growth. No new reward, learning or evolution admission follows from receiving them.

## Hampton integration and evidence limits
